J	Tan, J; Koohmishi, M; Jing, GQ; Kaewunruen, S; Guo, YL				Tan, Juan; Koohmishi, Mehdi; Jing, Guoqing; Kaewunruen, Sakdirat; Guo, Yunlong			Circular economy applications in railway infrastructure: A comprehensive evidence-based review	TRANSPORTATION GEOTECHNICS			English	Review						Railway track; Circular economy; 10R principles; Digital technologies; BIM; Digital twin	WASTE MANAGEMENT; DYNAMIC-BEHAVIOR; SUB-BALLAST; TRACK; CONCRETE; MAINTENANCE; DETERIORATION; OPTIMIZATION; TECHNOLOGIES; PERFORMANCE	Circular economy (CE) has emerged as a widely-recognized and vital paradigm across multiple sectors of the construction industry. Considering the complexity of ballasted and ballastless railway tracks, CE has remarkable potential to be employed throughout their whole lifecycle towards sustainability. This review paper examines the implementation of CE based on 10R principles-refute, rethink, reduce, reuse, repair, refurbish, remanufacture, repurpose, recycle, and recover-on all components of substructure and superstructure of ballasted and ballastless railway tracks, covering the potential applications from design and construction to maintenance and endof-life stages. In this context, promotion of CE in railway infrastructure can be achieved not only by enhancing the use of recycled materials and byproducts during the construction and maintenance stages, but also by leveraging digital information technologies. Tools such as material passport, 3D printing, and virtual reality, will further facilitate the implementation of CE principles from the earlier stages of the design lifecycle. Moreover, the state-of-the-art inspection technologies based on the remote sensing and Internet of things (IoT) can be employed to extend the lifespan of track components and apply optimized preventive maintenance activities based on asneeded repairs. In this context, digital platforms like geographical information system (GIS), building information model (BIM), and digital twin, enable the employment of these advanced technologies for promotion of CE based on 10R principles. The characterized strategies will pave the route to Net Zero by 2050 by involvement in the reduction of material quantities from design and construction efficiencies towards changing inspection and maintenance activities, highlighting reduction of CO2 intensity. J	Elshaboury, N; AlMetwaly, WM; Hesham, A; Abbas, A				Elshaboury, Nehal; AlMetwaly, Wael M.; Hesham, Amr; Abbas, Abdelrahman			Integrated BIM-GIS framework for holistic building stock assessment using 5D geo-modeling and digital twin concepts	JOURNAL OF BUILDING ENGINEERING			English	Article						Building stock; Demolition waste; Waste estimation; Urban mining; Building information modeling (BIM); Geographic information system (GIS)	DEMOLITION WASTE; CONSTRUCTION	Rapid urbanization has resulted in enormous quantities of demolition waste in recent years. Designing effective waste management strategies requires assessment of generated waste during the end-of-life stage of buildings; however, information on the quantity and composition of building stock is lacking. This paper presents a robust bottom-up approach using 5D geomodeling and digital twin concepts to predict building stocks and analyze their spatial distribution by leveraging the capabilities of building information modeling (BIM) and geographic information system (GIS). The material intensity and composition are estimated based on on-site investigations, laser scanning to capture detailed building geometry, and a BIM model to represent building components digitally. Additionally, the spatial distribution of building stock is modeled based on a GIS model. The proposed approach is demonstrated and verified in a case study of 23 typical residential buildings in an Egyptian district. The proposed BIM-GIS framework is a potential game-changer in the architecture, engineering, and construction industry. It thereby promotes effective waste management strategies across various districts by assessing their building stock. Non-structural cement uses, mainly mortars and finishes, account for 36% of total cement demand, ranging from 161 to 229 tons per building across the two case studies. The average savings per building corresponded to avoiding about 30 tons of steel, 165 m3 of ready-mix concrete, and 191 m3 of mortar, with net cost savings of about 3.15 million EGP per building. These results identify a practical pathway toward more sustainable, lower-carbon Egyptian residential buildings without increasing project cost.
