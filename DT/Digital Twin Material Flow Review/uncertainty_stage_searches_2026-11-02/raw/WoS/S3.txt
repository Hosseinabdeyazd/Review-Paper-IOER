To address the challenge of applying these methods to a wide range of buildings, the study proposes a framework using ontology graphs to integrate data from different systems, including FM, CMMS, BMS, and BIM. This study's results provide insight into the factors that influence occupant comfort, help to expedite identifying equipment malfunctions and point towards potential solutions, leading to more sustainable and energy-efficient buildings. Prognostics and Health Management (PHM) enables condition monitoring, fault diagnosis, and Remaining Useful Life (RUL) prediction, yet conventional centralized approaches face regulatory barriers, privacy risks, and difficulty in modelling heterogeneous sensor data, while incurring high communication costs at the edge. Federated Learning (FL) offers a decentralized and privacy-preserving paradigm for collaborative model training without raw data sharing. This review provides a comprehensive synthesis of PHM technologies, FL methodologies, and their integration for distributed, privacy-sensitive industrial environments. We first survey advances in physics-based, data-driven, and hybrid prognostic methods, together with intelligent maintenance and condition-based decision strategies, critically evaluating their adaptability under heterogeneous, resource-constrained settings. We then classify FL approaches by data distribution, network architecture, and participant type, and assess their applicability to PHM tasks through quantitative comparison of privacy-preserving techniques and lightweight deployment strategies. Building on this, we establish a structured mapping between PHM challenges and FL mechanisms, analyse applications in RUL prediction, fault diagnosis, and health monitoring, and identify unresolved gaps. The review concludes with a forward-looking roadmap covering domain-adaptive FL algorithms, resource-efficient edge architectures, and verifiable privacy mechanisms, as well as integration with emerging technologies such as large language models and digital twins.
