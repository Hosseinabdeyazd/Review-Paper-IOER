4.5. Locating uncertainty responsibilities within the synthesized DT structure

The design-to-use structure is a substantive synthesis output of this review (Figure 9), rather than background decoration. It separates the design brief (A), configuration choices (B), and functional allocation (C). The supporting studies inform particular concepts; their combination and allocation to the GeoAI–circular-material workflow are our synthesis. The structure connects two questions: which information must remain credible for a circular decision, and which implemented component is responsible for producing, transferring or checking that information (Xiong et al., 2025; Human et al., 2023; Agrawal et al., 2023).

Figure 9. The synthesized design-to-use structure retained as the organizing architecture. A specifies intended use; B selects configuration; C allocates responsibilities. Calibration, validation and diagnosis operate on implemented models and data. Reference keys are resolved in the supplementary architectural source register.

A specifies what uncertainty matters. The twinning object is a building, component or linked stock, with explicit spatial support and time horizon. The purpose determines the quantity of interest: material mass, release time, usable recovery or environmental consequence. Data requirements distinguish observed, inferred and assumed attributes, and separate fitting evidence from evaluation evidence. Service requirements identify who needs an uncertainty statement and when. Thus a district-scale stock estimate and a component-reuse decision may require different evidence even when they share source observations. These are proposed applications of the use-specific design brief, not universal accuracy thresholds (Shao and Helu, 2020; Agrawal et al., 2023).

Figure 10. The design brief fixes object, purpose, architectural intent, data requirements and services before configuration selection. In this review, these fields define the scope, evidence and authority under which uncertainty is assessed. The five-card arrangement is a synthesis, not a published universal taxonomy.

B determines the means of maintaining uncertainty information. Figure 11 retains all six configuration families. They are coexisting design dimensions, not successive material-flow stages. Their contribution is assessed by the operation they enable, not by assuming that a descriptor such as hybrid, federated or agent-based automatically supplies uncertainty quantification. The proposed responsibility map in Figure 12 therefore associates each family with an explicit operation and a checkable output.

Figure 11. Configuration vocabulary used in the architectural synthesis. Connecting curves indicate candidate pairings only. Dashed entries retain their unresolved evidence status. Agent-based simulation, autonomous software agency and agentic AI must not be treated as interchangeable.

The computational model performs inference and propagation. GeoAI may provide geometry, attribute probabilities or prediction errors; inventory models translate these into material quantities; lifetime models produce release trajectories; recovery models apply condition and yield; environmental models evaluate consequences. An uncertainty-bearing input must be carried through these transformations rather than replaced silently by its mean. Physics-based, data-driven and hybrid models can each support this operation when their uncertain inputs, parameters, discrepancy and dependencies are specified. A probabilistic graphical formulation provides one demonstrated basis for coupled state estimation and updating, but its transfer to the full material chain remains a synthesis proposition (Kapteyn et al., 2021).

The internal architecture makes that operation traceable. Modules expose input/output contracts; semantic identifiers connect observations, models and material entities; provenance records the origin and version of each assumption. This is the role of integration and knowledge representation, not a substitute for probabilistic computation (Kosse et al., 2025). System composition determines which buildings or components are combined. For a proposed aggregation of material masses, Var(ΣMᵢ)=ΣVar(Mᵢ)+2Σᵢ<ⱼCov(Mᵢ,Mⱼ); preserving shared archetype or material-coefficient dependencies is therefore important. This identity explains a design requirement, not a new empirical variance estimate.

Coupling and temporal organization determine whether estimates remain current. New survey evidence, renovation or recovery inspection can trigger a revision; provenance identifies downstream outputs requiring recalculation. Continuous updating is not necessary for every circular decision, and two-way exchange does not establish autonomous control. Agency determines who initiates checks, requests additional evidence, selects a model or approves an action. An agent can orchestrate these tasks, but the estimator and validation procedure supply the uncertainty evidence. Human review and automated actions may coexist. Agentic AI remains an unresolved configuration descriptor in this source set, rather than a required uncertainty-detection component (Kritzinger et al., 2018; Shao and Helu, 2020; Agrawal et al., 2023).

Figure 12. Proposed allocation of uncertainty responsibilities to the six configuration families. The operations are design requirements derived by connecting the architectural vocabulary to material-flow interfaces; they are not observed deployment frequencies. A defines acceptance requirements and C assigns each operation to a model, service or accountable role.

C assigns executable responsibilities and evidence gates. Detection compares an observation with its model prediction; a residual flags disagreement but does not uniquely identify its cause. Diagnosis examines data quality, parameter change, model form and operating context. Calibration updates selected parameters; validation checks performance against evidence appropriate to the intended use. Propagation carries a distribution, interval or scenario together with its dependencies into the next model. Tracking links each revision to affected downstream outputs. Decision support communicates the resulting consequence and whether available evidence satisfies A. These functions may be distributed across components; they are not additional S stages.

Applied to the four material interfaces, this allocation asks whether GeoAI uncertainty reaches material quantities (S1→S2), whether stock and lifetime dependencies reach release forecasts (S2→S3), whether condition and separation uncertainty reach recoverable yields (S3→S4), and whether recovery and substitution assumptions reach impact estimates (S4→S5). The DT contribution is the maintained relationship between these calculations, their evidence and their revision history. The selected material-flow evidence does not yet demonstrate this complete chain. The proposed architecture specifies what would have to be implemented and assessed to close that gap.
