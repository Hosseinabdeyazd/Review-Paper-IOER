Circular built-environment decisions connect spatial observations with material inventories, future outflows, recovery options and environmental impacts. Uncertainty is assessed within these activities, but its treatment across their interfaces remains difficult to trace. This review examines uncertainty continuity across five analytical stages (S1–S5) and synthesises digital-twin capabilities relevant to their connections. Searches in Scopus, Web of Science and IEEE Xplore support linked analyses of material workflows, uncertainty and digital twins. In the assessed uncertainty subset, 20 publications provide 23 interface assessments. Evidence is concentrated at S1→S2 and S2→S3; no assessment covers S3→S4, and only one directly addresses S4→S5. These findings describe the selected evidence rather than the complete literature, because broader retrieval and eligibility assessment remain open. The synthesis distinguishes uncertainty sources, analysis methods and documented propagation outcomes. It identifies complementary architectural responsibilities: computational models estimate and propagate uncertainty; information interfaces retain identities, dependencies and provenance; diagnostic and updating functions reassess validity; and decision interfaces communicate conditional results. These responsibilities connect digital-twin design choices with the information needed at material-flow boundaries. The contribution is an evidence-traceable architectural synthesis for examining uncertainty continuity, rather than an implemented or validated end-to-end digital twin.
