# 5. Discussion

## 5.1. Continuity is a property of interfaces, not the presence of UQ software

The central implication is that uncertainty-aware components do not automatically form an uncertainty-continuous workflow. GeoAI can produce an error score, a stock model can use variable material coefficients, and a lifecycle model can run Monte Carlo, yet these activities may refer to different uncertain inputs. The review therefore shifts the question from whether a method appears in a paper to whether the information needed by the next transformation survives the interface. Integrated stock–flow and impact workflows motivate this question, while the transition analysis specifies how it can be examined [U01,I35,I36,I37].

Three operations must be distinguished. Propagation computes consequences of specified uncertain inputs. Updating changes an uncertainty representation in light of observations or revised assumptions. Validation evaluates whether predictions and their uncertainty are adequate for the intended use. Running one operation does not establish the other two. In particular, a small prediction error does not establish calibrated predictive intervals, and a narrower posterior does not establish that missing mechanisms have been resolved. A digital twin is useful here because it can organise repeated observation, model revision and evidence evaluation, provided these operations are implemented explicitly [I33,R5].

## 5.2. What must cross each material-flow boundary?

At S1→S2, the transformation links inferred building attributes to material quantities. A schematic stock calculation is M = Σ_b A_b I_b, where A denotes a relevant geometric measure and I a compatible material-intensity coefficient. The uncertainty of the product depends on both factors and their dependence. Transferring only the predicted building class or mean coefficient loses information needed by the inventory model. A useful DT interface would retain class probabilities or alternative attribute states, material-coefficient uncertainty, consistent units and the aggregation rule. It should not turn a classification confidence score into a calibrated probability without supporting evaluation.

At S2→S3, an inventory is transformed into dated outflows using age, lifetime, renovation and event assumptions. A schematic mapping O(t) = Σ_c M_c f_c(t | age, scenario) emphasises that the output is a flow in a time interval rather than a stock at a date. The uncertainty representation must therefore carry material quantities together with the event-time model. A new building survey can update stock information, while a renovation event may revise remaining life and outflow timing. Revisions must preserve their relationship to the previous state rather than silently overwrite the forecast [U11,U12,U13,U18].

At S3→S4, potential outflow becomes technically recoverable and route-specific material. R = O × η × q is a deliberately schematic expression: η represents yield and q a quality/acceptance factor, and their meanings must be defined for the selected reuse or recycling route. Damage, contamination and selective-disassembly evidence may change these factors and their dependence. The current absence of a coded context at this boundary makes a condition-observation and recovery-evaluation interface a justified research priority, but not a validated solution. It would be inappropriate to assign universal recovery distributions to fill that gap [I20,I21].

At S4→S5, recovered materials acquire environmental consequences under substitution, allocation and system-boundary assumptions. A schematic impact calculation sums route-specific material quantities multiplied by environmental factors and subtracts eligible avoided burdens. The same recovered mass can lead to different results when substituted products, quality equivalence or future energy systems differ. A traceable interface must retain those conditions along with material quality and quantity. Circularity alone does not establish an environmental benefit, and a reported impact interval cannot be interpreted as end-to-end uncertainty unless upstream recovery uncertainty is included [U19,I22,I38].

These expressions illustrate interface requirements; the review has not fitted or executed a combined material-flow model. Their role is to make the architectural requirements precise enough for subsequent empirical testing.

## 5.3. How the twin could detect, track and explain uncertainty

The proposed architecture uses a common evidence contract across the four boundaries and allocates different checks to the functions that can perform them. The observation service checks completeness, calibration, coverage and timing. The inference service estimates states or parameters and records posterior or other supported uncertainty. The propagation service passes compatible samples, distributions or scenario identifiers through a stated transformation. The diagnostic service compares predictions with independent observations. The decision service expresses the consequences of the remaining uncertainty for an authorised choice. Identity, provenance and versions connect these services, while stakeholder roles determine who may revise assumptions, approve evidence or act on outputs [R2,R6,R15].

A useful diagnostic is a residual r = y_observed − y_predicted. A persistent or unexpectedly large residual can indicate a problem, but may originate in measurement noise, stale input, parameter error, model discrepancy or changed conditions. Diagnosis therefore requires assumptions about both observation and prediction. Under an appropriate predictive model, a standardised residual may divide r by the square root of predictive plus observational variance, including dependence terms where relevant. Such a diagnostic must not be presented as a universal detector or given an arbitrary threshold.

Tracking requires more than storing successive uncertainty widths. A change from a stock interval in tonnes to an impact interval in kilograms CO2-equivalent is a transformation in quantity and unit. Directly comparing the numerical variances would be meaningless. Comparisons of amplification or reduction require a common quantity, compatible scale and context, or a stated normalisation. Likewise, aggregation can reduce relative variability when errors offset while preserving a shared bias. These distinctions explain why the outcome sketches are a conceptual vocabulary, not numerical results for each stage.

The uncertainty representation should remain plural. Sampling or Bayesian inference is useful when probabilistic assumptions are supported; scenario comparisons remain appropriate when future alternatives do not have defensible probabilities. A qualitative limitation should be carried as a limitation rather than assigned an invented distribution. This allows a stakeholder to see both the calculated uncertainty and what the calculation has left outside its scope [I25,I33].

## 5.4. Architectural synthesis, scale and decision relevance

The novelty of the review lies in connecting three bodies of evidence through explicit interface requirements: material-flow uncertainty, domain-specific DT functions and general DT uncertainty mechanisms. It does not lie in inventing a new estimator, implementing a universal twin or demonstrating a reduction in environmental uncertainty. Direct evidence supports particular functions; compatibility reasoning links functions from different studies; proposals identify what still needs to be tested. The full architecture consequently remains a literature-informed, evidence-traceable synthesis.

The A–B–C design structure helps avoid treating every digital twin as the same technical object. A purpose such as stock estimation requires different observation frequency and validation evidence from an imminent recovery decision. A city-scale model and a component-level reuse assessment can share a record structure while requiring different spatial support, identifiers, uncertainty dependencies and validation data. This makes the design adaptable across scales; it does not establish scale-independent validity or computational scalability [R1,R5].

Stakeholders enter the architecture through information needs, authority and evidence responsibilities rather than a fixed list of organisations. Timing enters through valid time, update time, event triggers and decision horizon. A periodic or event-driven update can be suitable when the represented material state changes slowly; fast synchronisation alone cannot compensate for poor material information. The decision interface should report the quantity of interest, uncertainty representation, scenario conditions, evidence status and the reason for any recommendation [R5,R6].

## 5.5. Limits and research priorities

The strongest quantitative statements in this draft are reproducible frequencies within named coding sets. The archival 17-paper analysis and current 20-paper context analysis use different coding schemes and must not be combined as repeated samples. The former includes secondary and supporting-information-based evidence; the current eligibility ledger contains later targeted full-text checks, but does not retroactively establish every legacy outcome label. The general-DT subset is small and cross-domain. None of these distributions supports a population prevalence estimate or a ranking of uncertainty methods by effectiveness.

No pooled percentage reduction in uncertainty is reported. Such a synthesis would require extracting the quantity of interest, units, probabilistic assumptions, sample or scenario basis, uncertainty metric, upstream/downstream comparison and validation conditions from each eligible primary study. RMSE, coefficient of variation, confidence intervals, prediction intervals and scenario ranges cannot be pooled merely because all describe some form of spread or error. The existing extraction does not provide a harmonised basis for that operation.

The next empirical test should focus on one fully documented boundary before attempting the entire chain. It should compare deterministic transfer with a dependency-preserving alternative, retain identical input evidence, evaluate held-out observations where available, and test how changes affect the decision rather than interval width alone. Particular priority belongs to S3→S4 and to the connection between recovery quality and environmental substitution. Completing pending eligibility and page-specific evidence reconciliation remains necessary before this working manuscript can be submitted as a closed systematic review.

Taken together, the analyses suggest a precise role for digital twins: maintaining an inspectable relationship between uncertain observations, model transformations and decision consequences over time. The review supports this as an architectural direction, while leaving its end-to-end performance to future validation.
