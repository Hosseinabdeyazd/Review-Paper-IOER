## Eligibility

Exclude from the direct S1–S5 + DT + U intersection. Preserve as complementary stakeholder/measurement-quality methodology. The demonstrated contribution is a requirements elicitation process for predominantly operational energy/comfort services, not a material-stock, recovery or embodied-impact DT implementation. Mentioned building materials and LCA examples do not establish a demonstrated material-chain interface.

## Evidence

Section II: 37 stakeholders recruited; user stories collected through interviews; nine workshops extract more technical requirements; requirements are then translated into service definitions. Figure1 is a co-creation methodology rather than a runtime twin architecture.

Section III: proposed services cover sensor installation, energy performance, comfort/wellbeing, twin-based building/HVAC overview and forecasts, and dedicated data-quality monitoring. The latter is a distinct requested service, not just an implicit property of the sensors.

Section III.A: heterogeneous inputs include measurement uncertainty, sensor location and user interaction. The proposed quality service should both reduce and quantify output uncertainty. Monte Carlo, sensitivity analysis, pedigree matrices and fuzzy methods are described. The pedigree dimensions are reliability, completeness, temporal correlation, geographical correlation and technological correlation, scored1–5. No numerical uncertainty propagation experiment, fitted joint model, or validation of a deployed quality-aware twin is presented.

Section IV: results remain service needs and a measurement-centred co-creation methodology. Energy efficiency, costs and comfort are emphasized. The text supports positioning stakeholder requirements and a quality service in the synthesis architecture; it does not prove continuity through inventory, outflow, recovery and environmental-impact stages.

## Extraction boundary

Transferable proposed link: stakeholder need → required output quality → measurement/data-quality service → service decision. It is conceptual and requirements-based. Do not report the listed uncertainty methods as methods empirically applied and validated by this study.
