## Decision and scope

**Confirmed explicit uncertainty propagation, S5 with an S4 building-repair/retention bridge.** This is not a recovered-material yield or recyclability model. It predicts uncertain emissions of earthquake repair and replacement, including repairability/collapse decisions. A fixed 90% recycling assumption appears in waste treatment; recovery probability, salvage yield, quality loss, contamination and uncertain secondary-material substitution are not modeled. It should not be cited as demonstrating uncertainty propagation from recoverable material quantities into circularity credits.

The study expressly does **not** perform complete whole-building LCA (section2, p3). It adds earthquake repair as a use-stage module: production/construction for repair plus demolition, processing and disposal. Material factors consistently cover A1–A3; C1/C3/C4 are added separately. Transportation during construction and end-of-life is excluded, with variability and small contribution offered as reasons. No module-D avoided-production credit analysis is presented. Operational energy is not part of the modeled earthquake consequence.

## Actual transfer and transformation chain

1. **Material/geometry uncertainty to component repair carbon** (section4, pp5–10). For each component damage state, repair actions define physical material quantities. Random geometry changes those quantities; sampled carbon factors multiply them. Demolition and disposal contributions are added. Ten thousand Monte Carlo samples produce component consequence distributions summarized as lognormal medians and log dispersions. Dispersion is additionally increased for data-quality uncertainty.
2. **Hazard/response to damage** (sections3.1,5–6, pp4,10–13). Nine archetype RC frames are analyzed under hazard-consistent motions. FEMA P-58/PACT samples engineering-demand parameters (EDPs) from response analyses and component damage states through fragility functions.
3. **Damage to uncertain building carbon, with repairability branch** (section6.2, pp12–13). The previously derived component carbon consequence models transform sampled damage into repair emissions. Component outcomes are aggregated; collapse, large residual drift and a replacement threshold trigger whole-building replacement. One thousand carbon realizations per intensity level are used.
4. **Uncertain building carbon to annual risk** (sections3.1–3.2,6.2, pp4–5,13). Hazard integration yields mean annual frequency of exceeding each embodied-carbon amount; integrating the environmental loss curve yields expected annual environmental loss (EAL, kgCO2e/year).
5. **Detailed simulation to portable probabilistic surrogate** (sections3.2,7, pp5,14–16). Realizations are grouped by global building damage state using simulated maximum interstorey drift thresholds. Carbon is normalized by replacement carbon; ECDFs are fitted with Beta distributions. These damage-to-carbon (DS-CO2) distributions can be combined with building fragility and hazard without redoing component-level PACT simulations.

Equations1–3 (pp4–5): global fragility is a lognormal CDF; conditional carbon-loss exceedance is a mixture over discrete damage states, P(LR>=lr|IM)=sum_i P(LR>=lr|DS_i)P(DS_i|IM); annual exceedance integrates that vulnerability over the hazard measure. Equation5 (p8): services carbon is a weighted room/bathroom carbon-per-area average plus general-items carbon divided by dwelling area.

## Inputs and their uncertainty representation

**Carbon factors (Table1, pp5–6):** EPDItaly plus European sources where Italian data absent; over60 manufacturers and230 product classes. Means and standard deviations assembled across EPDs. Examples: concrete299±113 kgCO2e/m3; reinforcement729±94 kgCO2e/ton; clay bricks0.24±0.056 kgCO2e/kg; epoxy6.64±1.16 kgCO2e/kg; windows154.5±14.82 kgCO2e/m2. Some products have no available SD (e.g. shutters, radiators). The text says these factors are Monte Carlo sampled but does **not clearly name their probability-family assumption**, truncation or cross-material correlation. Do not silently label the factor inputs normal or lognormal: lognormal is explicitly assigned to the resulting consequence models, not clearly to these inputs.

**Infill geometry (section4.1.1, pp6–7):** independent/unrelated uniform wall heights2.50–2.75m and lengths4–5m. Windows fixed0.90×1.5m; doors fixed1.20×2.20m. Ten thousand random geometry/factor combinations; sample count selected using convergence sensitivity checks. Repair actions are defined by DS1 light cracking, DS2 extensive cracking/local brick failure, DS3 major crushing/spalling, generally replacement.

**Services geometry (section4.1.2, pp7–9):** ideal generic room and bathroom inventories, rather than measured room-by-room inventories. Generic room dimensions independent uniforms3–4.5m; minimum bathroom dimension uniform1.7–3m; bathroom area uniform3.5–5m2; bathroom share uniform0.05–0.10. Assumptions informed by Italian minimum dwelling rules. Ten thousand samples through equation5; services damage follows infill-wall damage.

**Structural geometry (section4.2, p10):** uniform beam widths0.25–0.40m, depths0.35–0.60m; column dimensions0.30–0.60m; floor heights3–4.5m; spans4–6m. Correlations omitted as considered low. Ten thousand samples of dimensions and factors map repairs to carbon. Repair operations include epoxy injection, concrete patching/replacement, rebar replacement and neighboring infill/slab work; brittle weak columns at DS3 are fully replaced.

## Dependence, conditioning and simplifying assumptions

- Wall/room/structural geometric correlations omitted (pp6–7,10). This suppresses dependence rather than estimates it.
- Services damage is linked to infill damage and uses the same fragility models (pp7,12).
- Within each performance group, component damage and resulting embodied-carbon outcomes are assumed uncorrelated (p12). This is important for aggregate uncertainty; do not imply fully correlated demolition/repair outcomes are modeled.
- Low/high-code Italian component fragilities were unavailable; US FEMA ordinary/special RC-frame fragilities substituted based on presumed similar behavior (p12). Pre-code/infill fragilities come from Italian experimental research.
- Replacement carbon is fixed at about385kgCO2e/m2 after augmenting303kgCO2e/m2 with services and end-of-life contributions. Values:374,220;623,700;997,920kgCO2e for3/5/8-storey buildings (p12).
- The replacement trigger is capped at60% of replacement value, plus collapse and residual-drift repairability relationships (pp12–13). The article does not report sensitivity of this threshold or uncertainties of replacement carbon.
- Fixed demolition factor3.4kgCO2e/m2 and processing/disposal0.013kgCO2e/kg; fixed90% recycling and10% landfill (p6). The Appendix's pedagogical worked example instead assumes100% landfill and zero construction waste (p18), unlike main simulations. Do not mix them.
- DS-conditioned Beta distributions **exclude collapse and excessive residual-drift cases**, because their global DS1–DS4 describe noncollapsed structures (p14). These replacement outcomes are included in the preceding full PACT environmental-loss assessment. Thus a portfolio user cannot simply treat the fitted distributions as covering all demolition/replacement risk without a separate branch.

Validation (section7, pp14–16): for the illustrated5-Pre case, surrogate vulnerability means differ from PACT stripe means by no more than about5% at a few stripes;16th/84th fractiles also visually comparable. Most DS2–4 Beta fits pass K–S at5%, but several require2–4%; most DS1 fits fail at5% and are non-rejected only with alpha as low as0.5–2% (Table11 includes5-Low DS1 at3%). Lowering alpha is weaker evidence, not a corrected fit. Authors acknowledge need for more low-intensity/DS1 data. Same-simulation agreement is not independent external validation.

Risk results (section6.2, pp13–14):3-High expected annual carbon57% lower than3-Pre;8-Low only16% lower than8-Pre. Low hazard levels dominate expected annual emissions because they occur frequently. Nonstructural damage dominates low intensity; collapse/residual drift dominate high intensity. Over assumed50years, averaged high-code damage carbon is about16% of initial construction; pre-code about27% in results text (conclusion says close to30%; publisher highlight28%); low-code conclusion24%. Preserve these rounding/context differences rather than asserting a universal28% value.

## Cost proxy and limits

Section8(pp16–17): normalized repair cost and carbon are nearly linearly correlated above loss ratio0.15 (reported rho0.99), largely because **the same demand/damage realizations and material quantities** feed both calculations. Below0.15, labor, technical effort and scaffolding drive costs without equivalent modeled carbon, so cost overpredicts carbon. DS1 mean proxy discrepancy approximately21–31%; DS2 about9%; DS3/4 under4%. A cost proxy may be useful for severe-event scenario analysis but biases annual-risk estimates dominated by frequent light damage. This correlation is not evidence of independent observations, causal equivalence, or general monetary-carbon conversion validity.

## Evidence strength and review use

Strong mechanistic evidence for probabilistic transfer across **damage/repairability → material intervention quantities → embodied environmental consequences**, with explicit input ranges, Monte Carlo sample counts, component distributions and building output distributions. The framework compresses rich simulations into portable DS-conditioned Beta distributions useful to urban stock models.

Limits: nine regular archetype frames, a single high-seismicity site; shared prior nonlinear analyses; uncertain carbon-factor distribution family not reported clearly; simplifying independence; borrowed fragility families; fixed recycling/treatment/replacement assumptions; omission of transport; partial rather than complete LCA; weak low-damage Beta fit; no measured post-earthquake carbon validation; no recovered-component market/supply/quality model; no reuse-yield uncertainty or substitution-credit propagation; no digital twin implemented. Retrofit optimization is proposed as future work rather than tested intervention portfolios in this article.

**Recommended evidence label:** full-text-confirmed explicit propagation, repair/retention-to-S5 environmental risk pathway; keep distinct from direct S4 material circular-recovery-to-S5 studies. Equation4 has been visually verified in publisher HTML and conflicts with the reported Table3 dispersions; the intended implementation remains unresolved.
